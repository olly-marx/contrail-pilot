## Part 1: FUTURE PLANES (8 steps, getting wilder)

### Key contrail and climate science for the high flyers (applies to steps 1-4)
- Supersonic fleets cruise in the stratosphere. Multi-model work finds they **add stratospheric NOx and
  water vapour, thin the ozone column, and add radiative forcing**. Flying lower or cutting NOx reduces the
  harm. (ACP 2024, https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/1743/2024/acp-24-1743-2024-relations.html)
- Size depends on design. A Mach 2.2 fleet at 17-20 km gave about 0.74% (about 2 DU) global ozone loss. A
  Mach 1.6 fleet gave about 0.046%. (RSC Environ. Sci.: Atmospheres 2022, https://pubs.rsc.org/en/content/articlehtml/2022/ea/d1ea00081k)
- Sulfur and soot aerosol from supersonic engines may **cool** more than the ozone, H2O and CO2 effects
  warm, so the net sign depends on fuel. SAF does not fix the NOx or water-vapour effects. (same RSC paper)
- Water vapour in the still, dry stratosphere lingers **about 10-15x longer** than lower down
  (researcher quoted in Aerospace America, https://aerospaceamerica.aiaa.org/could-booms-quiet-supersonic-technology-also-help-predict-contrails/).
- **Contrails themselves are rarer up there.** At 18-20 km, contrail formation is mainly limited to the
  tropics. The northern extratropical stratosphere is only cold enough in winter. IPCC 1999 assumed
  persistent stratospheric contrails were unlikely. (ACPD 2007, https://acp.copernicus.org/preprints/7/12927/2007/acpd-7-12927-2007.pdf)
- But they can happen. A contrail at 19 km over Darwin, at -82 °C and about 60% RH-ice, lasted about an
  hour. (ACP 2017, https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/17/2311/2017/)
- **Game takeaway:** high fliers make *fewer* visible contrails, but their invisible water vapour stays
  far longer and warms. The trail you can't see is the one that sticks around.

### Step F1: Concorde (history), REAL (retired 2003 [URL needed for retirement])
- Cruised at roughly 50,000-60,000 ft, typically about 55,000 ft at Mach 2, with a slow "cruise climb".
  Its maximum operating altitude was 60,000 ft. (https://flyawaysimulation.com/ask/answers/concorde-cruise-altitude/,
  secondary source. Also see PBS NOVA https://www.pbs.org/wgbh/nova/concorde/flyi-05.html)
- **Contrails/climate:** it flew above most ice-supersaturated air, so it made fewer persistent contrails.
  This is mostly inference from the stratosphere science above. I found no Concorde-specific study.
  Water vapour and NOx went into the stratosphere.
- **Kid line:** "The pointy rocket-plane that beat the Sun. Leave London at breakfast, arrive in New York
  *before* breakfast!" (clock-time claim from flight time minus the time-zone shift; [URL needed])
- **Fun fact:** passengers could see the curve of the Earth from cruise height (migflug,
  https://migflug.com/afterburner/?p=175707, blog source).

### Step F2: Boom XB-1 / Overture, PROTOTYPE (XB-1) and STUDY (Overture)
- XB-1 went supersonic (Mach 1.122, about 35,290 ft) on 28 Jan 2025. It was the first independently
  developed jet to break the sound barrier. (https://boomsupersonic.com/press-release/boom-achieves-supersonic-flight)
- Overture: 64-80 passengers, Mach 1.7, up to 100% SAF, with Symphony engines of about 35,000 lb thrust
  (same press release and https://spectrum.ieee.org/supersonic-jet). It has not flown.
- **Contrails/climate:** SAF cuts lifecycle CO2 but **not** the NOx or water vapour put into the
  stratosphere (RSC 2022 above). Mach 1.6-1.7 fleets fly lower than Concorde-style Mach 2.2 designs and
  modelled ozone loss is much smaller.
- **Kid line:** "A shiny new speed-jet. Faster than Concorde's grandkids, but the sky remembers its steam."
- **Fun fact:** XB-1 is a small one-pilot demonstrator, not a passenger plane.

### Step F3: NASA X-59 "quiet boom", PROTOTYPE
- First flight 28 Oct 2025, a subsonic shakedown. It is designed to cruise at Mach 1.42 with a soft
  "thump" instead of a boom, thanks to its very long nose. (https://www.sciencealert.com/nasas-x-59-quiet-supersonic-jet-just-aced-its-first-flight ;
  https://avweb.com/aviation-news/nasa-x-59-makes-its-historic-first-flight/)
- Later envelope expansion flights in 2026 are reported (https://airdatanews.com/nasas-x-59-flies-with-landing-gear-retracted-for-the-first-time).
  Check NASA for its current supersonic status.
- **Contrails/climate:** a quiet boom is about *noise*, not climate. It would still put water and NOx up high.
- **Kid line:** "A plane with a nose so long the pilot can't see out the front, and has to use a camera!"
  (camera "eXternal Vision System": [URL needed])
- **Fun fact:** NASA plans to fly it over towns and ask people how loud it sounded.

### Step F4: Hypersonic hydrogen airliner (LAPCAT A2, Mach 5), STUDY
- Reaction Engines' A2 was an EU-funded LAPCAT study (not ESA-led, though ESA coordinated). It was
  designed for 300 passengers at Mach 5 on liquid hydrogen with Scimitar precooled engines, Brussels to
  Sydney in about 4-5 hours. It was never built. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LAPCAT ;
  https://www.universetoday.com/12701/british-engineers-design-hypersonic-passenger-jet)
- History rung: SR-71 Blackbird, REAL [URL needed].
- **Contrails/climate:** a 2022 ACP study of a hydrogen hypersonic fleet found **8-20x the surface warming
  of a subsonic reference aircraft, despite zero CO2**. Almost all of it came from the extra stratospheric
  water vapour, with a small ozone effect. (https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/22/14323/2022/acp-22-14323-2022.html)
  This is the best teaching moment in the branch.
- **Kid line:** "Zero carbon, but it breathes out a cloud of steam where the sky can't wash it away."
- **Fun fact:** the engine uses super-cold hydrogen fuel to chill the scorching incoming air before it
  reaches the engine.

### Step F5: Truss-braced wing (Boeing/NASA X-66) and SUGAR Volt, STUDY (X-66 was a planned PROTOTYPE, now paused)
- X-66 was a modified MD-90 with long thin braced wings, due to fly in 2028. Boeing **paused** it on
  24 Apr 2025 to focus on thin-wing ground tests. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_X-66 ;
  https://www.militaryaerospace.com/commercial-aerospace/article/55287731/nasa-and-boeing-pause-experimental-x-66-development)
- SUGAR Volt: a Boeing-for-NASA hybrid-electric truss-braced concept, STUDY [URL needed].
- **Contrails/climate:** the green direction is *lower and slower*: less fuel per seat, subsonic altitudes,
  and you can still steer around ice-supersaturated regions. Efficient engines run cooler exhaust, which
  can make contrails form slightly *more* easily (the Schmidt-Appleman criterion) [URL needed].
- **Kid line:** "Wings like a glider's, held up by stilts. It sips fuel like a hummingbird."
- **Fun fact:** the wings are so long they need braces, like a table propped up with sticks.

### Step F6: Solar high-flyer (Solar Impulse 2), REAL (historic flight, curiosity)
- Flew around the world on sunlight alone, 9 Mar 2015 to 26 Jul 2016, in 17 legs. Batteries overheated
  in Hawaii and grounded it for about a year. (https://livescience.com/55552-solar-plane-completes-flight-around-the-world.html ;
  https://www.aviationtoday.com/2016/07/26/solar-impulse-2-completes-fuel-less-round-the-world-flight/)
- **Contrails/climate:** no combustion, so **no exhaust water and no contrail**. It could only carry one
  pilot, so it cannot scale to airliners.
- **Kid line:** "A plane powered by sunshine. It flies forever, but very slowly, and only fits one person."
- **Fun fact:** the pilot flew solo for days and took short naps in the air.

### Step F7: Nuclear-powered aircraft, REAL as history (reactor carried, never propelled)
- The US NB-36H flew 47 times in 1955-57 carrying a working reactor of about 1 MW. The reactor **never
  powered the engines**. The crew sat in a shielded capsule. The Soviet Tu-95LAL was a parallel effort.
  Both countries gave up because of weight and danger. The US programme was cancelled in 1961.
  (Blog sources: https://migflug.com/afterburner/convair-nb-36h-nuclear-reactor-bomber-cold-war/ ;
  https://www.xataka.com/otros/convair-nb-36h-enorme-bombardero-estrategico-que-volaba-reactor-nuclear.
  Confirm with National Museum of the USAF [URL needed].)
- A nuclear-*propelled* airliner is FICTION-leaning. The shielding is too heavy, and a crash would scatter
  radioactive material.
- **Contrails/climate:** a direct-air-heated reactor engine burns no fuel, so it makes no exhaust water and
  no contrail (physics inference).
- **Kid line:** "A 1950s bomber that carried a real nuclear reactor, plus a lead-lined room for the crew."
- **Fun fact:** the reactor came along for the ride but never moved the plane an inch.

### Step F8: Fusion-powered airliner, FICTION
- Why: no fusion device on Earth has yet produced net electricity. Experiments are building-sized, need
  huge magnets, and make neutron radiation that would need heavy shielding. A flyable compact fusion reactor
  has no demonstrated path. (General physics. Cite ITER or NIF status pages before showing this in game
  [URL needed].)
- **Contrails/climate:** in-game, make it contrail-free (no combustion), but label it "dream physics".
- **Kid line:** "A tiny star in a box, powering a plane. Scientists can barely keep a star going in a
  building-sized machine, so this one is pure imagination."
- **Fun fact:** fusion fuel comes from seawater (deuterium), which is why everyone dreams about it.

(An optional bonus rung is a blended-wing plane or hydrogen airliner, but those are already on the real ladder.)

## Most fun ideas
1. **The hydrogen hypersonic twist:** zero CO2 but 8-20x warming. A perfect "wait, what?" moment.
2. **The invisible trail:** stratospheric fliers make fewer contrails, but their steam lingers 10-15x longer.
3. **NB-36H passenger reactor:** a real plane that carried a reactor that never powered it.
4. **The "panic button" Autoland gadget:** a real 2020 feature where a passenger presses a button and the plane lands itself.
5. **The X-59 periscope cockpit**, where the pilot can't see out the front.
6. **The magnet ladder**, from a horseshoe to a superconducting magnet to a tractor beam, which the kids will love.
7. **The WVSS-II sensor:** a real laser on airliners that measures the exact thing that makes contrails.
